A little history

Why it is called Ugolki

Уголки means “corners.”

The exact origin is uncertain. Sources commonly place early forms in Britain or Europe in the 18th century. The game belongs to the same broad family of corner-to-corner race games as Halma, while Russia popularized compact versions on an ordinary 8×8 board.

This app teaches the 3×3, nine-piece version: classic orthogonal movement, no captures, and optional multi-jumps.

History is deliberately phrased conservatively because surviving accounts disagree on the precise origin.

The strategic trick

A piece is not only a runner. It is also infrastructure. Strong play builds chains of “stepping stones” that let later pieces cross several squares in one turn. Sometimes the best move is not the longest jump now; it is the move that keeps the highway useful for the next pieces.

Rules in 30 seconds

Goal: fill the opponent’s original 3×3 camp with all nine of your pieces.

Move: either step one empty square horizontally/vertically, or jump over an adjacent piece into the empty square beyond.

Chains: after a jump, you may continue jumping with the same piece and may change direction. You can stop whenever you want. You may jump over either color. Nothing is captured.
